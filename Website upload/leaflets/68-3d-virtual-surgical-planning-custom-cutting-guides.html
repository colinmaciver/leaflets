3D Virtual Surgical Planning & Custom-Made Cutting Guides

Using computer modelling of your own scans to plan your reconstruction in advance and make bespoke surgical guides and plates

This leaflet is a general guide. Your own surgeon and OMFS clinic team will discuss the details of your specific operation, as every case is different. Please write down any questions and bring them to your next appointment.

1. What is 3D virtual surgical planning and why is it used?

For complex reconstructions — most often jaw reconstruction using bone from the fibula or hip — your surgical team can use computer software to plan the operation in detail before you ever go to theatre. This is called 3D virtual surgical planning (VSP). Your own CT scan is used to build an accurate 3D model of your jaw and the bone to be used for reconstruction, allowing your surgeon to plan exactly where bone cuts should be made and how the reconstruction should be shaped, before the operation begins.

From this virtual plan, custom-made cutting guides and, where appropriate, custom plates are then manufactured specifically for your anatomy. These are used in theatre to help your surgeon reproduce the planned bone cuts and positioning accurately. This planning is not a treatment in itself — it is a preparation step that supports your main reconstructive operation (such as a fibula or DCIA free flap), making that operation more precise and, in many cases, quicker.

2. What does the planning process involve for you?

This stage happens before your operation and does not itself involve an anaesthetic.

  • You will have a CT scan (and sometimes additional photographs or measurements of your face and bite) in the weeks before your operation, specifically for planning purposes.
  • This scan data is used to build a detailed 3D computer model of your jaw and the bone that will be used to reconstruct it.
  • Your surgical team plans the operation on this virtual model, working out the exact position of bone cuts and how the reconstruction will be shaped and positioned.
  • Custom surgical guides — and, where needed, custom plates — are then designed from this plan and manufactured (often 3D printed) ready for your operation.
  • Your surgeon will usually be able to show you images of the planned reconstruction beforehand, which can help you understand what to expect from your operation.

3. How is it used during your operation?

  • The custom cutting guides are fitted to the bone during your operation, allowing your surgeon to make the planned cuts precisely, in the positions worked out during virtual planning.
  • Where a custom plate has been made, this is used to help secure the reconstructed bone in the planned position.
  • The rest of your operation — for example, the flap harvest, transfer, and the blood vessel reconnection using loupe magnification — proceeds in the usual way for the procedure you are having; the details of that operation are covered in its own leaflet (for example, the Fibula or DCIA free flap leaflets).
  • Using pre-planned guides and plates in this way is intended to make the reconstruction more accurate and can shorten the time needed for this part of the operation.

Your main operation is carried out under general anaesthetic. Before your operation, you will have an anaesthetic assessment to check your fitness for a general anaesthetic — this is also your opportunity to discuss any specific questions or concerns about the anaesthetic.

4. What to expect and what results to hope for

3D virtual surgical planning is intended to make your reconstruction more predictable and accurate, and in many cases to reduce the time needed in theatre for the bone-shaping and fixation part of your operation. Published data on this technology reports a reduction in operating time of up to around 45 minutes on a reconstruction case, and very close agreement between the planned and actual position of the reconstructed bone (within a fraction of a millimetre on average) when guides are used as planned. The remainder of your recovery follows the same course as for the main operation you are having (for example, your fibula, DCIA, or other free flap leaflet), since this planning process supports that operation rather than replacing any part of your aftercare.

5. Possible risks and limitations

The risks of your surgery itself are those of the main reconstructive operation being planned (see the relevant procedure-specific leaflet, for example Fibula or DCIA free flap). Risks and limitations specific to the planning and guide-making process itself are uncommon, but include:

  • A need to repeat your planning CT scan if the original images are not detailed enough for accurate planning.
  • Occasionally, the manufactured guide or plate does not fit as expected once in theatre, meaning your surgeon needs to adapt the plan during the operation using their own judgement and experience, as would be done without VSP.
  • A small delay to your surgery date is occasionally needed to allow time for scanning, planning, and manufacture of custom guides and plates, particularly if changes to the plan are needed.
  • As with any technology, virtual planning is a tool to support your surgeon's own expertise and decision-making, not a replacement for it — your surgical team remains able to adapt the plan during your operation if your anatomy or findings on the day require it.

Questions patients often ask

Is this a separate operation?

No. The planning itself involves a CT scan and computer modelling before your surgery date, with no anaesthetic of its own. The custom guides and plates it produces are then used during your main reconstructive operation, which has its own separate anaesthetic and procedure, covered in that operation's own leaflet.

Why is this used for my reconstruction?

It allows your surgical team to plan the exact position of bone cuts and the shape of your reconstruction in detail beforehand, using your own scan data, which can improve the accuracy of the reconstruction and, in many cases, shorten the time needed in theatre for this part of your operation.

Will I be able to see the plan beforehand?

Often, yes — your surgeon can usually show you images of the planned reconstruction from the virtual model, which can help you understand what to expect.

What if my anatomy looks different once the surgeon is operating?

Your surgeon's own judgement and experience always takes priority. If something differs from the plan once in theatre, your surgical team can adapt as needed, in the same way surgery would be adapted without this technology.

Does this delay my operation?

The planning process needs to happen in the weeks before your surgery to allow time for imaging, virtual planning, and manufacture of custom guides and plates. Your team will build this into your overall treatment timeline.

Who can I contact if I have concerns?

For concerns about your reconstruction itself once you are home, please also refer to the specific leaflet for your main operation (for example, Fibula or DCIA free flap). Use the contact details below.
