Botox Injection for TMJ / Masseter Muscle Pain

Using botulinum toxin (Botox) injections to relax an overactive jaw muscle and ease jaw pain, clenching, and related headaches

This leaflet is a general guide. Your own surgeon and OMFS clinic team will discuss the details of your specific operation, as every case is different. Please write down any questions and bring them to your next appointment.

1. Why has this been recommended?

The masseter is one of the main muscles used for chewing, at the angle of your jaw. In some people, this muscle becomes overactive — often linked to clenching or grinding the teeth (bruxism), which is frequently worse at night or during periods of stress. An overactive masseter can contribute to jaw ache, temple or ear-area pain, tension-type headaches, and temporomandibular joint (TMJ) symptoms, and can sometimes cause the muscle itself to become enlarged (see the separate TMJ Dysfunction leaflet for more on this condition generally).

Botulinum toxin (Botox) injected into the masseter muscle temporarily weakens it, reducing how forcefully it contracts. This can ease muscle-related jaw pain and clenching-related symptoms, and is often used alongside other conservative measures for TMJ dysfunction such as a bite splint, anti-inflammatory gel, and physiotherapy, rather than as a first-line treatment on its own.

2. What does the appointment involve?

This procedure does not usually require a general anaesthetic and is normally carried out as a short appointment in clinic, using a fine needle. Most patients tolerate the injections well without any numbing, though your surgeon can discuss options for your comfort if needed.

3. What does the procedure involve?

Using a sterile technique, botulinum toxin is injected directly into the masseter muscle on each side of the jaw. A typical total dose is around 100 units, split evenly between the left and right masseter muscles (approximately 50 units per side), though your surgeon will confirm the dose planned for you. The injections themselves take only a few minutes.

4. What to expect afterwards

  • You should remain upright and avoid rubbing the injected area for 2 hours afterwards, to reduce the chance of the toxin spreading to nearby muscles.
  • It typically takes 7 to 10 days for the full effect to develop, so you should not expect an immediate change in your symptoms.
  • Most patients notice a gradual reduction in jaw clenching, muscle tenderness, and related headaches or jaw ache as the effect builds.
  • The effect of botulinum toxin is temporary, generally lasting in the region of 3 to 4 months, after which the muscle gradually regains its usual strength and repeat treatment is often needed to maintain the benefit.
  • You should contact the OMFS clinic if you notice any concerning or unexpected effects in the weeks after treatment.

5. Possible risks and side effects

Botulinum toxin injection for the masseter is generally well tolerated, and the great majority of patients experience only mild, temporary effects. Possible risks include:

  • Bruising, tenderness, or a small lump at the injection site, which usually settles within a few days.
  • Temporary weakness or asymmetry of nearby muscles if the toxin spreads slightly beyond the masseter — for example, some patients notice mild, temporary asymmetry of the smile if the injection is close to muscles around the mouth.
  • Difficulty chewing tougher or chewier foods if the masseter is weakened more than intended — this is usually mild and temporary, resolving as the effect wears off.
  • Headache, which is usually mild and short-lived.
  • A less-than-expected or shorter-than-expected effect, particularly with the first treatment, which can sometimes be adjusted with a different dose at a future session.
  • Rarely, an allergic reaction to the injected material.

Questions patients often ask

Will this stop me from being able to eat normally?

Most patients continue to eat normally, though some notice mild, temporary difficulty with tougher or chewier foods while the effect is strongest. This usually settles well before the treatment wears off.

How soon will I notice a difference?

It typically takes 7 to 10 days for the full effect to develop. You should not expect an immediate change on the day of injection.

How long does the effect last?

The effect is temporary, generally lasting around 3 to 4 months, after which the muscle gradually regains its usual strength. Many patients have repeat treatments to maintain the benefit.

Will my face look different?

Some patients notice a subtle softening of the jawline over time if the masseter muscle reduces in bulk with repeated treatment, but the primary aim of this treatment is easing muscle-related pain and clenching, not a cosmetic change.

Do I need to do anything differently on the day?

You should remain upright and avoid rubbing the injected area for 2 hours after your appointment. Otherwise, you can generally resume normal activities straight away.

Who can I contact if I have concerns after my appointment?

Please contact the OMFS clinic if you notice any concerning or unexpected effects. Use the contact details below.
