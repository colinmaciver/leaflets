Genioplasty (Chin Surgery)

Patient information leaflet

This leaflet explains the operation to change the position or shape of the chin and what to expect. Your surgeon, orthodontist and OMFS clinic team will talk it through with you.

At a glance
  • Genioplasty moves the chin bone to a better position so that the face is in better balance.
  • It is done from inside the mouth under general anaesthetic, so there are no visible facial scars.
  • The chin is held with small titanium plates and screws.
  • Most people stay in hospital overnight.
  • The lower lip and chin are numb for a time. A small number of people have lasting numbness.

1. Why has this operation been recommended?

The profile of your chin needs correction because the bones of your face and jaws are out of balance. A chin that is too small, too large, too far back or crooked can change how the face looks. The operation to change the position of the chin is called genioplasty.

Genioplasty is often done at the same time as other jaw surgery. Your surgeon may instead advise doing it at a later stage. Please see our overview leaflet on orthognathic (jaw) surgery.

2. The anaesthetic

The operation takes place under general anaesthetic, so you will be completely asleep. You must not drive or operate machinery for 48 hours afterwards.

Before your operation, you will have an anaesthetic assessment to check your fitness for a general anaesthetic — this is also your opportunity to discuss any specific questions or concerns about the anaesthetic.

3. What does the operation involve?

The operation is done from inside your mouth, so there are no visible scars on the skin of the face. A cut is made through the gum on the inside of the lower lip to reach the lower jaw and chin. The chin bone is then cut with a small saw. It is moved into its new position, which may be forward, back, up, down or to one side. It is held there with small metal plates and screws. The gum is closed with dissolvable stitches that can take a fortnight or longer to disappear.

The plates and screws are usually left in place permanently. They are made of titanium, which does not set off metal detectors at airports.

4. What to expect afterwards

  • Hospital stay. Most people stay overnight. The position of the chin is checked with an X-ray before you go home.
  • Pain. It is not usually a very painful operation, but it will be sore. Regular painkillers will be arranged, and you will be sent home with painkillers and a course of antibiotics. The discomfort is usually worst for the first few days and may take a couple of weeks to go.
  • Swelling and bruising. Your chin will feel swollen and tight. Swelling and bruising are variable and are generally worst on the second or third day. Cold compresses and sleeping propped up for a few days help. Most swelling goes within a fortnight. Some slight swelling can last several months, although usually only you and your family will notice.
  • Eating. If the chin is operated on alone, you can start with a soft diet and gradually build up to normal food over a few days.
  • Time off. Most people need around a week off work if the chin is operated on alone. This varies with your job.
  • Review. An appointment will be arranged to see your surgeon and orthodontist.

5. Possible complications

Complications with this type of surgery are uncommon and may not happen to you. Published percentages are not available for all of them, so they are described in words.

  • Bleeding. Some oozing inside the mouth on the night of the operation is normal. Significant bleeding is very unusual. If it happens, press on the area for at least 10 minutes with a rolled-up clean handkerchief or gauze.
  • Numbness. Your lower lip will be numb and tingly, like after a dental injection. It may take several months to go, and in a minority of patients it may last for ever.
  • Infection. The plates and screws can occasionally become infected and need to be removed. If this happens it is not normally a problem until several months after the surgery.
  • Chin position and shape. The result may not be exactly as planned, or the chin may be uneven. Further surgery is occasionally needed.
  • Lower teeth. Rarely, the roots of the lower front teeth are affected or the gum on the lower front teeth recedes.

Questions patients often ask

Will I have a scar?

No. The cut is inside the lower lip.

Can I eat normally afterwards?

Start with a soft diet and gradually return to normal food over a few days.

Will the plates set off airport metal detectors?

No. They are titanium and do not.

Will my lip stay numb?

The numbness usually goes over several months. In a small number of people it stays.
