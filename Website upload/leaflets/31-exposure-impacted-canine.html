Exposure of an Impacted Canine

Patient information leaflet

This leaflet explains why an impacted upper canine needs a small operation to help it come through, and what to expect. Your surgeon, orthodontist and OMFS clinic team will talk it through with you.

At a glance
  • The canine (eye tooth) normally comes through between 11 and 13 years.
  • If it is in the wrong place it will not come through on its own.
  • The operation is short and is usually done under a day-case general anaesthetic.
  • Your orthodontist then gently brings the tooth into line using a chain, plate or pack.
  • Soreness is mild and swelling is usually slight.

1. Why has this operation been recommended?

The canine, or eye tooth, normally comes through into the mouth between the ages of 11 and 13. Sometimes one or both canines develop in the wrong position. They often lie across the roof of the mouth, behind the front teeth. This is called an impacted canine.

As part of your orthodontic treatment, the tooth needs help to come through. If it is left alone it will not come through normally. It may also damage the roots of the front teeth or push them out of position.

2. The anaesthetic

The operation is usually done under a day-case general anaesthetic. You are fully asleep and go home the same day. You must not drive or operate machinery for 48 hours afterwards.

Before your operation, you will have an anaesthetic assessment to check your fitness for a general anaesthetic — this is also your opportunity to discuss any specific questions or concerns about the anaesthetic.

3. What does the operation involve?

While you are asleep, the gum lying over the canine is pushed back. Sometimes a little of the bone around the crown of the tooth also has to be removed. The method your orthodontist will use to pull the tooth into place is agreed with you beforehand. One of three things is done under the same anaesthetic:

  • Bracket and chain. A tiny bracket is glued to the tooth and a fine chain is attached. Your orthodontist uses the chain to pull the tooth into position. The chain is stitched out of the way, but it is delicate, so be careful when eating for the first few weeks.
  • A plate. A small window is cut in the gum and a plastic plate is fitted over the area, held by clips on the back teeth. Wear it all the time except when cleaning your teeth. If you leave it out, the gum can grow back over the tooth.
  • A pack. A gauze pack soaked in antiseptic is placed over the tooth and held with stitches. It is removed after a few weeks. Do not dislodge it. If it comes out, contact the department for advice.

Sometimes stitches are used to hold the gum in place. These are usually dissolvable and take about two weeks to disappear.

4. What to expect afterwards

  • Pain. The procedure is not particularly painful but the mouth will be sore. Your surgeon will arrange painkillers.
  • Swelling. There is usually very little swelling.
  • Antibiotics. These are not usually needed.
  • Cleaning. Brush gently and rinse with a mouthwash or warm salt water from the day after surgery. Avoid biting on hard food around the area.
  • Review. You will need to be seen a few weeks later by your orthodontist or surgeon. More orthodontic appointments follow as the tooth is moved into place, which can take many months.

5. Possible complications

This is a minor operation and serious problems are uncommon. Good published percentages are not available, so the descriptions below are in words.

  • Soreness, mild swelling and bruising (common, settles within days).
  • Bleeding from the gum (uncommon). Press on the area with a clean damp gauze for 10 minutes. If it does not stop, contact us.
  • Infection (uncommon).
  • The chain or bracket coming loose, or the pack or plate coming out. Contact us or your orthodontist.
  • The gum growing back over the tooth, particularly if a plate is not worn.
  • Occasionally the tooth does not move as hoped, or damage to the nearby tooth roots, and further treatment may be needed.
  • Rarely, the tooth cannot be brought into position and removal is discussed.

Questions patients often ask

Will my tooth be pulled out?

No. The aim is to keep the tooth and bring it down into the dental arch.

How long will the tooth take to move into place?

This varies. It often takes many months of orthodontic treatment.

Can I go back to school or work?

Most people need a couple of days. Avoid contact sports until you are told it is safe.
