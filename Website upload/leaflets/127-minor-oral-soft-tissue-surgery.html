Minor Oral Soft-Tissue Surgery (Frenectomy, Mucocele, Fibroma and Biopsy)

Patient information leaflet

This leaflet explains common small operations on the gums, lips, cheeks and tongue. Your surgeon and OMFS clinic team will talk it through with you.

At a glance
  • These are small operations on the soft tissues of the mouth, not the teeth or jaw bones.
  • They include releasing a tight tongue or lip tie (frenectomy), removing a cyst-like swelling (mucocele), removing a firm lump (fibroma or epulis) and taking a biopsy.
  • Most are done under local anaesthetic and take 15 to 30 minutes.
  • Any tissue removed is sent for examination under the microscope.
  • The area is sore for a few days and heals within 1 to 2 weeks.

1. Why has this operation been recommended?

  • Frenectomy. A frenum is a small fold of tissue that joins the lip or tongue to the gum or floor of the mouth. If it is tight (for example tongue-tie) it can limit tongue movement, affect feeding or speech in children, cause a gap between front teeth, or pull on the gum. The fold is released or trimmed.
  • Mucocele. A soft, bluish or clear swelling, usually on the inside of the lower lip, caused by a blocked or injured minor salivary gland. It often bursts and returns. Removal of the swelling and gland stops it coming back.
  • Fibroma or epulis. A firm, smooth lump of scar-like tissue, often caused by long-term rubbing or irritation, for example from a tooth, denture or cheek biting. Removal stops it catching and being sore.
  • Biopsy. A small piece, or all, of an ulcer, patch or lump is taken to find out what it is. This is the only way to be sure. See leaflet on oral mouth biopsy for more detail.

2. The anaesthetic

Most of these are done under local anaesthetic, an injection that numbs the area while you stay awake. In children, anxious patients or more extensive surgery, sedation or a general anaesthetic may be offered. After sedation or general anaesthetic you need an adult with you and must not drive for 24 or 48 hours respectively.

Before your operation, you will have an anaesthetic assessment to check your fitness for a general anaesthetic — this is also your opportunity to discuss any specific questions or concerns about the anaesthetic.

3. What does the procedure involve?

  • The area is numbed.
  • The swelling, lump or tight band is cut out or released using a scalpel, and sometimes a laser or diathermy (heat) to limit bleeding.
  • The edges are closed with a few dissolvable stitches. Sometimes a small raw area is left to heal by itself.
  • The tissue removed is sent to the laboratory to be looked at under the microscope.

The procedure usually takes 15 to 30 minutes.

Occasionally a lump turns out to be a cancer once it has been fully examined under the microscope. If this happens, your case will be carefully discussed at the head and neck multidisciplinary team (MDT) meeting, where specialists from different fields agree on the most appropriate plan, and this will then be explained to you in person before any further treatment goes ahead.

4. What to expect afterwards

  • Going home. You go home the same day.
  • Pain. The area is sore for a few days. Paracetamol, with ibuprofen if you can take it, is usually enough.
  • Eating. Soft, cool food. Avoid spicy, hot, acidic or sharp foods for several days.
  • Mouth care. Rinse gently with warm salt water after meals, and keep brushing your teeth. A mouthwash may be advised.
  • Bleeding. A little oozing is normal. If it continues, press on the area with a clean damp swab for 10 minutes. Contact us if it does not stop.
  • After a frenectomy. Gentle tongue or lip exercises may be advised to stop the tissue tightening again.
  • Healing. Most areas heal in 1 to 2 weeks. A white or yellow film over the area is normal.
  • Results. You will be told the result at a follow-up appointment.

5. Possible complications

  • Pain, swelling and bruising (common, short-lived).
  • Bleeding (uncommon).
  • Infection (uncommon).
  • A swelling coming back. This happens with some mucoceles. Removing the gland lowers the chance but cannot remove it completely. A frenum can reattach or tighten.
  • Numbness or tingling in the lip, tongue or chin if a small nerve is bruised. This is uncommon and nearly always temporary.
  • Damage to a salivary duct, particularly under the tongue (rare).
  • A scar in the mouth, which is usually unnoticeable.

Questions patients often ask

Will the lump come back?

Fibromas rarely do once the cause is removed. Mucoceles sometimes do. Your surgeon will explain.

Is a biopsy painful?

The area is numbed first, so you should feel pressure but not pain. It is sore afterwards for a few days.

How soon will I get a result?

Usually within one to two weeks.

Can I eat afterwards?

Yes, once the numbness has worn off. Choose soft, cool foods.
