Suturing (Stitching) of Facial Lacerations

Patient information leaflet

This leaflet explains what to expect if you have a cut on your face that needs to be stitched under general anaesthetic by your surgeon and OMFS clinic team.

At a glance
  • Your cut is too large or complex to be glued or taped, so it needs stitching in layers.
  • The stitching is done while you are fully asleep.
  • Deeper stitches dissolve. Skin stitches are removed at a clinic appointment, usually about 5 to 7 days later.
  • Swelling and bruising are common, and settle in a few days. Sleeping propped up helps.
  • Most people go home the same day or the next day.
  • The scar is red at first and fades over many months. Scar revision is possible later if needed.

1. The problem

You have a cut (laceration) on your face that needs stitching. The doctor who examined you decided that it is too big or too deep to be closed with glue or tape. It may also be near the eye, lip, nose or ear, or contain dirt that needs thorough cleaning, or be in a place where you could not keep still for local anaesthetic. For these reasons, a general anaesthetic (being put fully to sleep) allows the surgeon to clean and repair it carefully, with the best chance of a good scar.

Small cuts can often be stitched in the Emergency Department or clinic under local anaesthetic. This leaflet is about the operation under general anaesthetic.

2. The anaesthetic

The operation is done under general anaesthetic, so you will be completely asleep. Before your operation, you will have an anaesthetic assessment to check your fitness for a general anaesthetic — this is also your opportunity to discuss any specific questions or concerns about the anaesthetic.

Local anaesthetic is often injected around the wound as well, to ease pain when you wake up. You must not eat or drink before the operation for the time you are told. If you have a tetanus risk, you may be offered a tetanus injection.

3. What does the operation involve?

When you are asleep, the wound is washed out and cleaned, and any dirt or damaged tissue is carefully removed. The wound is usually closed in layers. First, stitches are placed under the skin to hold the deeper tissues together. These deeper stitches are made of dissolvable material and do not need to be removed. Then the skin surface is stitched with fine stitches.

Your surgeon will confirm which type of stitch is used on the skin of your face. Skin stitches on the face are often fine stitches that are removed at a clinic visit, while some surgeons use dissolvable stitches. At the end, a dressing may be put over the wound.

4. What to expect afterwards

  • Pain. The wound is unlikely to be very sore. Regular painkillers are arranged. Any discomfort is usually worse for the first few days.
  • Swelling and bruising. The amount depends on where the cut is. It is most noticeable around the eyelids and can last about 48 hours or longer. Sleeping propped upright for the first few days helps.
  • Hospital stay. Depending on the size of the wound, you may go home the same day or stay overnight. You may be given antibiotics in a drip to prevent infection, and be sent home with painkillers and a course of antibiotic tablets if needed.
  • Stitches and follow-up. An appointment will be made to remove skin stitches and review you in the outpatient clinic. This is usually about 5 to 7 days after surgery. Keep stitches and dressings dry until then.
  • Time off. Depending on your job, you may need about a week off and should avoid strenuous exercise. You must not drive or use machinery for 48 hours after a general anaesthetic.
  • Scar care. Once healed, protect the scar from the sun and massage it as advised. See our leaflet 'Looking After Your Wound and Scar'.

5. Possible problems

  • Bleeding is unlikely to be a problem. If it happens at home, press firmly over the site with a clean, rolled-up handkerchief or swab for at least 10 minutes. If it does not stop, contact us or go to the Emergency Department.
  • Redness of the scar. The wound looks red for several weeks, even after the stitches are removed. This is normal. Later, there may be a little discomfort when the deeper stitches dissolve, a few months after surgery.
  • Infection. This is uncommon with good cleaning and antibiotics when needed. Contact us if the wound becomes more painful, hot, red or swollen, or discharges pus.
  • Scar appearance. All cuts leave a scar. A small number of people are unhappy with the final appearance, and scar revision (a further small operation) can be considered. This is not normally done until at least 6 months after the original stitching, because the scar takes that long to settle.
  • Thick scars. Occasionally, people develop an excess of scar tissue (hypertrophic scar or keloid). This is unusual and may need extra treatment, for example silicone gel, injections or surgery.
  • Damage to deeper structures, such as facial nerve branches or the tear duct, is possible with deep cuts, and your surgeon will check for this and explain if it applies to you.

Questions patients often ask

Will I be left with a scar?

Yes. Every cut that goes through the skin leaves a scar, but careful stitching and good aftercare give the best result. It fades over many months.

When will the stitches come out?

Skin stitches are usually removed at about 5 to 7 days at a clinic visit. Your surgeon will confirm your timing.

Can I wash my face?

Keep the stitches and dressing dry until they are removed, unless your surgeon says otherwise. Gently clean around the area as advised.

When should I call?

If you have bleeding that will not stop, or the wound becomes more painful, red, swollen or starts to discharge, or you develop a fever.
