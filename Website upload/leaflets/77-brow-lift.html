Brow Lift

Patient information leaflet - endoscopic, coronal, temporal, direct and pretrichial techniques

This leaflet explains what a brow lift does and the different ways it can be done, so that you can talk it through with your surgeon and OMFS clinic team.

At a glance
  • A brow lift raises drooping eyebrows and smooths forehead frown lines. It can also relieve a heavy, tired look of the upper eyelids.
  • There are five main techniques: endoscopic, coronal (open), temporal (lateral), direct and pretrichial. Your surgeon will recommend the one that suits your face and hairline.
  • Expect forehead swelling and bruising for 1 to 2 weeks, and numbness or itching of the scalp for weeks to months.
  • Results last for years but the brow will continue to age.

1. Why a brow lift has been suggested

With age, the eyebrows drop and the forehead skin loses its stretch. This can give a tired, sad or angry look, and a heavy upper eyelid. The forehead muscles often work harder to hold the brows up, which can add forehead lines and tension.

A brow lift raises the brows to a more youthful position. It is often combined with eyelid surgery (blepharoplasty) or a facelift. Lifting the brow first can mean less upper eyelid skin needs to be removed. It is also used when facial palsy has caused one brow to droop; your surgeon will discuss this if it applies to you.

Wrinkle-relaxing injections (botulinum toxin) can lift the outer brow slightly for a few months and are an alternative for mild cases (see the botulinum toxin and fillers leaflet).

2. The anaesthetic

Endoscopic, coronal and pretrichial brow lifts are usually done under general anaesthetic. Direct and limited temporal lifts can often be done under local anaesthetic with or without sedation.

Before your operation, you will have an anaesthetic assessment to check your fitness for a general anaesthetic - this is also your opportunity to discuss any specific questions or concerns about the anaesthetic.

You will not be able to drive or operate machinery for 48 hours after a general anaesthetic.

3. What the operation involves

The aim of every technique is the same: to release the tissues of the forehead and brow and to fix them in a higher position. The techniques differ in where the cuts are made, how much of the forehead is treated and how long the scars are.

The operation usually takes 1 to 2 hours. Your surgeon will confirm the technique that has been chosen for you.

Stitches and dressings

Stitches under the skin are dissolvable. Skin stitches in the hairline or above the brow are sometimes non-dissolvable and are removed after about 7 to 10 days. Your surgeon will confirm which type of stitch is used. A light head dressing may be applied. A small drain is occasionally used; if so, it is usually removed within 24 hours.

4. What to expect afterwards

  • Most people go home the same day or the next day.
  • Sleep with your head raised for the first week.
  • Swelling and bruising of the forehead and eyelids are common. Swelling may move down towards the eyes. Most settles within 1 to 2 weeks.
  • Numbness, tingling or itching of the forehead and scalp is common and may last for weeks or months.
  • Avoid heavy lifting, bending and strenuous exercise for about 2 to 3 weeks, or as advised.
  • Most people return to desk work after 1 to 2 weeks.
  • You will be seen in clinic after about a week for a wound check.

Once the wounds have healed, gentle moisturiser massage can help scars settle, and sun protection (SPF 30 to 50) protects them from darkening. The final position of the brows is judged at about 3 months.

5. Possible complications

Brow lift is generally safe. Reliable percentage figures are limited and vary between techniques, so the words below are qualitative.

  • Swelling, bruising, tightness and headache: expected.
  • Numbness or tingling of the forehead and scalp: common and usually temporary, but sometimes long lasting.
  • Scarring: the length and position depend on the technique. Scars can be wide or visible.
  • Hair loss or thinning along the scar: uncommon, may be permanent.
  • Bleeding and haematoma (blood collection) or infection: uncommon. A haematoma may need draining.
  • Weakness of forehead movement (frontal branch of the facial nerve): uncommon. Usually temporary, resolving in weeks to months. Permanent weakness is rare.
  • Asymmetry or an over-lifted, surprised look, and brows that drop again over time.
  • Eyelids that do not close fully or dry eyes: if the brows are lifted too much. Usually temporary.
  • Dissatisfaction or need for further surgery: possible.
Contact the clinic or the Emergency Department urgently if you have rapidly increasing swelling or pain, a fever, spreading redness or any change in your vision.

Questions patients often ask

Will I look surprised?

The aim is a natural look. Over-lifting is avoided, and your surgeon will plan the height carefully with you.

Which technique is best?

It depends on your brow position, hairline, forehead height and skin. The table in section 3 is a general guide.

Will my hairline change?

Coronal and pretrichial lifts can alter the hairline height: coronal may raise it, pretrichial lowers the forehead. Endoscopic and temporal lifts have little effect.

How long does it last?

Usually several years. The brows continue to age. Results from direct lifts tend to be the most lasting.

Is it an alternative to eyelid surgery?

Sometimes. If the heavy upper lid is caused by a low brow, lifting the brow treats the cause. Often both are done together.
