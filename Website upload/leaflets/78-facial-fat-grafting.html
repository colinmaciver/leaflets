Facial Fat Grafting

Patient information leaflet (short)

This short leaflet explains facial fat grafting (fat transfer) so that you can talk it through with your surgeon and OMFS clinic team.

At a glance
  • Your own fat is taken from another part of the body (such as the tummy or thighs), prepared and injected into the face to restore volume.
  • It can be used for cosmetic rejuvenation, to improve scars or to rebuild hollow areas after injury, surgery or conditions that cause shrinking of the face.
  • Some of the fat does not survive, so a repeat treatment is sometimes needed.
  • Swelling and bruising settle over 2 to 3 weeks.

1. Why fat grafting has been suggested

As we age the face loses fat and looks hollow or tired. Fat can also be lost after injury, surgery, radiotherapy or conditions such as Parry-Romberg syndrome. Fat grafting adds volume back using your own tissue. It also contains cells that may improve the quality of the overlying skin and scars.

It is often used with other operations such as a facelift or facial reanimation surgery, or on its own.

2. The anaesthetic

Fat grafting is usually done under general anaesthetic, because fat is collected from one part of the body and injected into another. Small treatments can sometimes be done under local anaesthetic with sedation.

Before your operation, you will have an anaesthetic assessment to check your fitness for a general anaesthetic - this is also your opportunity to discuss any specific questions or concerns about the anaesthetic.

You will not be able to drive or operate machinery for 48 hours after a general anaesthetic.

3. What the procedure involves

  • Collecting the fat: through one or two tiny cuts, a small amount of fat (often 20 to 60 mL, about a few tablespoons) is gently collected from the abdomen, thighs or another area using a fine tube (liposuction).
  • Preparing the fat: the fat is washed and cleaned so that only healthy fat tissue is used. Our unit uses a closed system called Lipogems. It rinses the fat with saline and breaks it into very small clusters using gentle mechanical processing, without chemicals or enzymes. The aim is a smoother, finer product that is easier to inject and may survive better.
  • Injecting the fat: tiny amounts are injected through very small skin punctures using fine, blunt-tipped tubes, in many thin layers. The surgeon places it where volume is needed, such as the cheeks, temples or around the eyes.
  • The small cuts are closed with dissolvable stitches or glue. Tiny scars are usual.

The procedure usually takes 1 to 2 hours. In most cases you go home the same day.

4. What to expect afterwards

  • The treated areas look swollen at first, partly because the surgeon slightly overfills, and partly because of swelling. You will look fuller than the final result.
  • Bruising and swelling settle over 2 to 3 weeks. The donor area will be sore and bruised, like a deep bruise, for a few weeks. Your surgeon will tell you whether a support garment is needed.
  • Do not press, massage or lie on the treated areas for the first 2 to 3 weeks. Sleep on your back with your head raised.
  • Most people return to desk work after 1 to 2 weeks.
  • The final result is seen at around 3 to 6 months.

You will be seen in clinic after about 1 to 2 weeks. A repeat treatment can be done after several months if more volume is wanted.

5. Possible complications

  • Pain, swelling and bruising at the donor and treated areas: expected.
  • Fat loss (resorption): a proportion of the fat does not survive. The amount varies a lot between people and areas. Published estimates range widely, and you should expect that a good part of the fat may be lost. Because of this, more than one treatment is sometimes needed.
  • Infection of the fat graft or donor site: uncommon.
  • Lumps, irregularities, asymmetry or too much fill, sometimes with small firm lumps (fat necrosis or cysts). Most settle; some need treatment.
  • Further fat grafting may be needed.
  • Dissatisfaction with the result.
  • Blocked blood vessel in the face with skin damage or loss of sight: very rare but serious. It has been reported when fat is injected into certain areas (glabella, nose), which is why surgeons use blunt tubes and small amounts.
Contact the clinic or the Emergency Department urgently if you have severe pain, spreading redness, a fever, or any sudden change in your vision.

Questions patients often ask

Why use my own fat?

It is natural, it does not cause an allergic reaction and it can improve the quality of the skin. The disadvantage is that some fat is lost, and there is a second area of treatment (the donor site).

What is Lipogems?

Lipogems is a closed system that rinses and gently breaks down the fat into very small clusters without chemicals or enzymes. The prepared fat is then injected.

Is the result permanent?

The fat that survives usually lasts for years, but it behaves like fat anywhere in the body and can change with weight gain or loss. The face keeps ageing.

How many treatments will I need?

Often one, sometimes more. Your surgeon will discuss this.
