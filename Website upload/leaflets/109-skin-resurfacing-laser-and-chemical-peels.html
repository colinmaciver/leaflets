Skin Resurfacing: Laser Resurfacing and Chemical Peels

Patient information leaflet

This leaflet explains laser resurfacing and chemical peels, treatments planned by your surgeon and OMFS clinic team to improve the quality and texture of facial skin.

At a glance
  • Both treatments remove the damaged top layers of skin so that new, smoother skin forms.
  • They can improve fine lines, sun damage, uneven colour, scarring and some acne scars.
  • Light treatments need little downtime. Deeper treatments need days to weeks of healing.
  • Strict sun protection before and after is essential.
  • Risks include pigment change, infection, scarring and redness that lasts. Smoking and some medicines increase risk.

1. Why has resurfacing been recommended?

With age, sun and smoking, skin develops fine wrinkles, rough texture, uneven colour and dark patches. Scars from acne or injury can also leave the skin uneven. Resurfacing treats the surface with a controlled injury, so that the body replaces it with new skin. It does not lift sagging skin or replace a facelift.

Your surgeon will assess your skin type, the problem, your medical history and your expectations to advise on the best treatment, which may include no treatment, creams or a combination.

Who may not be suitable

  • people who are pregnant or breast-feeding
  • those who have taken isotretinoin (a strong acne tablet) in the recent past, usually within the last 6 to 12 months (your surgeon will advise)
  • active skin infection or cold sores, or a history of frequent cold sores that is not covered by antiviral medicine
  • a tendency to keloid or very thick scars
  • very dark skin, where some lasers and deeper peels carry a greater risk of colour change
  • recent sun tan or strong sun exposure, or smoking, which affects healing

2. The anaesthetic

The choice depends on the treatment. Light peels and gentle lasers need no or only numbing cream. Medium peels and most resurfacing lasers are done under numbing cream, local anaesthetic injections, with or without sedation. Deeper or full-face treatment may be done under general anaesthetic.

If you are to have a general anaesthetic: Before your operation, you will have an anaesthetic assessment to check your fitness for a general anaesthetic — this is also your opportunity to discuss any specific questions or concerns about the anaesthetic.

3. What do the treatments involve?

Preparation

  • You may be asked to use skin-preparing creams, such as a retinoid or a lightening cream, for some weeks beforehand.
  • You may be given antiviral tablets to prevent a cold sore flare-up.
  • Avoid sun, sunbeds and fake tan for several weeks beforehand, and stop smoking.
  • Please tell us about all medicines, allergies, previous cosmetic treatments and cold sores.

Chemical peels

A chemical solution is applied to the skin and left on for a set time, then neutralised or removed. Light (superficial) peels (for example glycolic or salicylic acid) cause mild peeling over a few days. Medium-depth peels (for example trichloroacetic acid) reach deeper and cause several days of redness, swelling and peeling, with healing in about 1 to 2 weeks. Deep peels (for example phenol) are rarely used, need careful monitoring including the heart, and take a longer time to heal. You may feel stinging or burning during the peel.

Laser resurfacing

A laser delivers light energy to the skin. Ablative lasers (such as CO2 or erbium) vaporise the top layer of skin and tighten the layers below. They give the biggest improvement but need the longest healing. Non-ablative or fractional lasers treat tiny columns of skin and leave the rest intact, so healing is faster but more sessions are often needed. You and the team wear protective eye shields throughout. Laser treatment tends to cause less bleeding than a blade, and the skin can look raw or charred at first. This is normal, as it is for laser treatment elsewhere.

4. What to expect afterwards

  • Light peels and fractional lasers: redness, tightness and flaking for 3 to 7 days. You can usually go back to work in a few days, using make-up if allowed.
  • Medium peels and ablative lasers: swelling, oozing and crusting, and redness for 1 to 2 weeks, often longer for deeper treatment. New skin is pink and may stay pink for weeks to months. Plan for around 1 to 2 weeks off.
  • Keep skin moist with the ointment you are given. Do not pick crusts or peeling skin, as this can scar.
  • Cleanse gently and avoid scrubs, harsh products and make-up until healed.
  • Sun protection is essential. Avoid direct sun and use a high-factor sunscreen (SPF 30 to 50) every day, plus a hat, for several months. New skin is easily burnt and darkened.
  • Pain is usually mild to moderate. Simple painkillers are normally enough. Cool compresses and sleeping propped up reduce swelling.
  • Avoid vigorous exercise, saunas, swimming pools and smoking until healed. See our leaflet 'Looking After Your Wound and Scar'.
  • Results appear as the skin heals, and continue to improve for 3 to 6 months. Some people need more than one treatment.

5. Possible complications

  • Redness and swelling lasting longer than expected, usually settling with time.
  • Pigment change. Darkening (hyperpigmentation) is the commonest problem, particularly in darker skin or without sun protection. Lighter patches (hypopigmentation) can occur and are sometimes permanent, mainly after deeper treatments.
  • Infection. Bacterial, fungal or cold sore (herpes) infection can occur. Contact us promptly if there is pain, pus, a rash or blisters.
  • Scarring. Uncommon with careful treatment but more likely with deeper treatment, infection, picking or isotretinoin use.
  • Milia (small white spots) and acne flares during healing.
  • Sensitive skin or uneven result, and, rarely, burns from the laser or a strong reaction to the peel.
  • Eye injury, which is why eye shields are always used.
  • Disappointment. Results are not guaranteed and are limited by your skin.

Our treatments are planned carefully to keep these risks low, but we do not quote exact percentages because they vary a lot with the type, depth and your skin.

Questions patients often ask

Does it hurt?

Light treatments sting a little. Deeper treatments are done with numbing or anaesthetic, and afterwards the skin feels sore and hot for a day or two.

When can I wear make-up?

After light peels, often within a day or two. After deeper treatment, once the skin has healed. We will advise you.

How long do the results last?

Several years, but ageing and sun exposure continue. Sunscreen and good skin care help to maintain the result.

Can I have it in summer?

Treatment is best planned for when sun exposure can be limited, and some laser and peel treatments are better in cooler months.
